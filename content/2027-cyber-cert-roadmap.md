# The 2027 Cyber Cert Roadmap

**Which certification you get first, what order to take the rest in, and the job each one actually leads to.**

A free guide for career changers and absolute beginners. Written by a working cybersecurity professional specializing in identity governance and access control.

Facts verified 9 November 2026 against official certifying-body sources. Pricing and exam requirements change; always confirm on the certifying body's own site before you book.

---

## How to read this guide

Every page separates two different things:

- **FACT**: exam code, length, passing score, recommended experience, renewal requirement. Taken from the certifying body's own website, verified 9 November 2026.
- **RECOMMENDATION**: the order, the track to pick, when to book. That is judgement, not fact. Your situation may differ.

---

## The 30-second version

You do not need every certification. You need the right three, in the right order, with a job title in mind.

**Step 0 (optional): CompTIA Network+ (N10-009).** Only if networking is completely foreign to you. If you already troubleshoot networks, skip it.

**Step 1: CompTIA Security+ (SY0-801).** The anchor. Every track below starts here.

**Step 2: pick one track by the job you want, not by the cert that sounds coolest.**

| Track | Leads to | Certification sequence |
|---|---|---|
| The Defender (SOC) | SOC Analyst | Security+ then CySA+ then SecAI+ |
| The Attacker (Offensive) | Junior Penetration Tester | Security+ then PenTest+ |
| The Cloud | Cloud Security Analyst | Security+ then AWS Cloud Practitioner then Solutions Architect Associate then Security Specialty |
| The Identity | IAM Analyst | Security+ then SC-300 or Okta Certified Professional then SailPoint |
| The Auditor (GRC) | GRC Analyst / IT Audit Analyst | Security+ then GRCP or GRCA then CGRC then CISA |

**Step 3: go deep or go senior.** Deep means a specialization (AI security, cloud security, identity). Senior means CompTIA SecurityX, which is the capstone for people already doing the work.

If you only do one thing this year: earn Security+ and build two lab projects you can talk about.

## Start here: CompTIA Security+ (the anchor)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Certification | CompTIA Security+ |
| Current exam | SY0-801 (V8) |
| Launch date | Expected November 17, 2026 |
| Questions | Maximum 90, multiple choice and performance based |
| Duration | 90 minutes |
| Passing score | 750 on a scale of 100 to 900 |
| Formal prerequisite | None listed by CompTIA |
| Recommended experience | Security administrator with two years of hands-on experience |
| Validity | Three years from the date you earn it |
| Renewal | Continuing education through CompTIA's CE program, or a qualifying higher-level CompTIA certification |
| Language | English |

**Leads to:** SOC Analyst (Tier 1), Security Administrator, IT Security Specialist, Junior Systems Administrator, and a step up from help desk.

**Why it is first.** It is the only certification on this list that appears as a baseline requirement across defensive, offensive, cloud, and identity job postings. It is also the one credential that makes a career changer look like a security hire instead of an IT hire.

**RECOMMENDATION: sit SY0-801, not SY0-701.** CompTIA retires the previous English version a few months after a new version launches; confirm the exact SY0-701 retirement date on comptia.org. If you plan to test in the second half of 2027, study the V8 objectives from the start instead of switching versions mid-prep.

**Study time to plan for:** 8 to 12 weeks at 8 to 10 hours a week if security is new to you. Less if you already work in IT.

## Track 01: The Defender (SOC)

**Leads to:** SOC Analyst Tier 1, Cyber Defense Analyst, Incident Response Analyst, Security Administrator.

This is the highest-volume entry path. Most people who say "I want to get into cybersecurity" end up here first, because every organization with a security team needs someone watching alerts.

### CompTIA CySA+ (CS0-004)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Current exam | CS0-004 (V4), launched June 23, 2026 |
| Questions | Maximum 85 |
| Duration | 165 minutes |
| Passing score | 750 on a scale of 100 to 900 |
| Recommended experience | About four years in a SOC analyst or vulnerability analyst role |
| Validity | Three years |
| Renewal | Renewed through CompTIA continuing education |

**What it signals.** CySA+ is the certification hiring managers read as "this person can work a detection queue, triage an alert, and write it up." It is the natural second step after Security+ and the one that separates a candidate from a stack of Security+ resumes.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Do not wait for four years of experience before sitting it. The recommended experience describes the job you are targeting, not a gate. Six to twelve months of real exposure plus serious lab work is enough to prepare honestly.

### CompTIA SecAI+ (CY0-001)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Current exam | CY0-001 (V1), launched February 17, 2026 |
| Questions | Maximum 60 |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Passing score | 600 on a scale of 100 to 900 |
| Recommended experience | Three to four years in IT and two or more years in cybersecurity |
| Validity | Three years |
| Renewal | Renewed through CompTIA continuing education |

**Why it belongs in a 2027 plan.** Security operations work is being rebuilt around AI-assisted detection, and this is the first CompTIA certification aimed directly at securing AI systems and using AI inside security operations. It is new, so it is not yet a common job requirement. It is a differentiator, not a ticket.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Treat SecAI+ as your third cert if you are aiming at a modern SOC or detection engineering role. Skip it if you have not worked a queue yet.

### Capstone: CompTIA SecurityX (formerly CASP+)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Current exam | CAS-005 (V5), launched December 17, 2024 |
| Questions | Maximum 90 |
| Duration | 165 minutes |
| Passing score | Pass or fail, no scaled score |
| Recommended experience | Ten years of hands-on IT, including five in security |
| Validity | Three years |
| Renewal | Renewed through CompTIA continuing education |

**RECOMMENDATION.** Not a 2027 goal for a beginner. Put it on the five-year horizon, after you hold a senior analyst or security engineer title.

**The path in plain terms:** help desk or IT support, then SOC Tier 1, then SOC Tier 2, then detection engineer, incident response, or security engineer.

## Track 02: The Attacker (Offensive)

**Leads to:** Vulnerability Analyst, Junior Penetration Tester, Security Consultant, Application Security Analyst.

CompTIA PenTest+ (PT0-003)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Current exam | PT0-003 (V3), launched December 17, 2024 |
| Questions | Maximum 90, multiple choice and performance based |
| Duration | 165 minutes |
| Passing score | 750 on a scale of 100 to 900 |
| Recommended experience | Three to four years in a penetration testing role, with Network+ and Security+ knowledge |
| Validity | Three years |
| Renewal | Renewed through CompTIA continuing education |

**RECOMMENDATION: this is not an entry-level cert, even though beginners are drawn to it.** Offensive work is a second job, not a first one. The people who get hired as junior testers usually arrive from a SOC, systems administration, or development background, because a tester who cannot explain how a defender sees an attack is not useful on an engagement.

**RECOMMENDATION.** If offensive security is the goal, do this: Security+ first, then two years in a defensive or infrastructure role while you build a public lab portfolio, then PenTest+. That sequence is slower and it is what actually converts.

**Version check before you book.** CompTIA states that PenTest+ exams usually retire about three years after launch, which puts a replacement around 2027. Confirm the live exam code on comptia.org before you buy a voucher.

**Skills that matter more than the badge:** report writing, a repeatable methodology, and one deep specialization (web applications, Active Directory, or cloud).

## Track 03: The Cloud

**Leads to:** Cloud Support Associate, Cloud Security Analyst, Cloud Security Engineer, DevSecOps support.

Cloud security is the highest-paying of the five tracks and the one where a certification alone moves your resume fastest, because the work is tied to a specific platform.

### AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner (CLF-C02)

**FACT**: 65 questions, 90 minutes, 100 USD, valid three years, no prerequisite. Designed for candidates with up to six months of AWS exposure, including people from non-technical backgrounds.

### AWS Certified Solutions Architect - Associate (SAA-C03)

**FACT**: 65 questions, 130 minutes, 150 USD, valid three years. AWS recommends at least one year of hands-on experience designing cloud solutions, and notes that candidates with one to three years of IT experience have used it as a starting point.

### AWS Certified Security - Specialty (SCS-C03)

**FACT**: 65 questions, 170 minutes, 300 USD, valid three years. Intended for people with five years of IT security experience and two or more years securing AWS workloads.

**RECOMMENDATION.** For a security career, the useful sequence is Cloud Practitioner, then Solutions Architect Associate, then Security Specialty. Security Specialty is the one that changes your title, but it assumes you can already design in AWS, which is what the associate exam forces you to learn.

**FACT worth knowing.** Once you hold one active AWS Certification, AWS gives you a 50 percent discount on your next AWS exam.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Cloud plus Security+ beats five vendor-neutral certs with no cloud exposure. Pick one cloud and go deep.

## Track 04: The Identity (IAM)

**Leads to:** IAM Analyst, Identity and Access Administrator, IAM Engineer, SailPoint or Okta Administrator.

Identity is the quiet track. It has less competition than SOC work, it is central to zero trust programs, and it rewards people who like process, data, and access reviews. Most IAM engineers learned on the job, which means a certification plus a homelab is a real opening.

### Microsoft Certified: Identity and Access Administrator Associate (SC-300)

**FACT**: Intermediate level, focused on Microsoft Entra ID, hybrid identity, and identity governance. Microsoft renews this certification every 12 months through an online assessment, with no exam retake. Exam price is set by country and region.

**RECOMMENDATION.** The strongest single IAM credential if the employers you are targeting run Microsoft 365 and Entra. It pairs naturally with Security+ and is the most common IAM requirement you will see in job posts.

### Okta Certified Professional, then Okta Certified Administrator

**FACT**: A first attempt costs 250 USD, Okta lists no formal prerequisite for the Professional exam, and certification is valid for two years. Administrator requires Professional first.

**RECOMMENDATION.** Take this if the companies you want use Okta for workforce identity. It is a platform credential, so it is worth more when the job posting names the platform.

### SailPoint Certified Identity Security Administrator and SailPoint Certified IdentityIQ Engineer

**FACT**: Role-based certifications from SailPoint University, including Identity Security Administrator and IdentityIQ Engineer. SailPoint publishes the current fee and recommended experience for each path; confirm both on its own site before booking.

**RECOMMENDATION.** SailPoint is one of the largest dedicated identity governance vendors, and its certifications show up directly in IAM job requirements. Best taken once you are inside an IAM team that uses the platform, or after a serious lab build.

### CIDPRO (Certified Identity Professional, IDPro)

**FACT**: Vendor-neutral IAM certification from IDPro, 750 USD, multiple-choice, delivered worldwide through IDPro's testing partner.

**RECOMMENDATION.** The right choice if you want one credential that is not tied to a single vendor, for example for identity governance, IAM program, or consulting work. It is knowledge-based, so pair it with hands-on platform work.

## Track 05: The Auditor (GRC and IT audit)

**Leads to:** GRC Analyst, IT Audit Analyst, Security Compliance Analyst, Risk and Controls Analyst, IAM Compliance Analyst.

This is the track most roadmaps leave out, and it is one of the few security jobs you can enter without having configured the tooling yourself. The work is evidence: control testing, sample selection, access reviews, walkthroughs, and writing findings. You are not the person who builds the system. You are the person who can tell whether it is designed, operating, and evidenced. For a career changer with no hands-on IT background, it is often the most realistic first security job.

### OCEG GRC Professional (GRCP) and GRC Auditor (GRCA)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exam | GRCP: 100 scored questions (plus up to 15 unscored), 2 hours, 70 out of 100 to pass, open book, no AI tools |
| GRCA | 100 questions, 2 hours, 70 out of 100 to pass, open book |
| Prerequisites | None for either credential |
| Cost | Bundled in the OCEG All Access Pass; the nonrenewing Pro All Access Pass is listed at 875 USD |
| Maintenance | Active Pro All Access Pass and 8 CPE hours per year across all OCEG certifications |

**RECOMMENDATION.** GRCP is the fastest credible entry credential on this page. It has no experience gate, the exam is open book, and the material teaches a vocabulary for governance, risk, and control that you will use in interviews. Add GRCA if audit and assurance is the specific job you want.

### ISC2 CGRC (Certified in Governance, Risk and Compliance)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exam | 125 items, 3 hours, passing score 700 on a scale of 1,000 |
| Domains | Seven, from governance and risk through control selection, implementation, assessment, compliance, and maintenance |
| Experience | Two years in one or more of the seven domains. Without it, you can become an Associate of ISC2 and gain the experience within three years |
| Fee | See ISC2 |
| Maintenance | 60 CPE credits over three years and 135 USD annual maintenance |

**RECOMMENDATION.** CGRC is the closest thing to a pure GRC credential. The two-year requirement is not a gate to sitting the exam; it is a gate to the full certification. Take it after Security+ and after you have touched real control or audit work, not before.

### ISACA CISA (Certified Information Systems Auditor)

**FACT**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Exam | 150 multiple-choice questions, 4 hours, passing score 450 on a scale of 200 to 800 |
| Fee | 575 USD for ISACA members, 760 USD for non-members, plus a 50 USD application fee |
| Experience | Five years of IS audit, control, assurance, or security work. Up to three years can be waived for degrees and certifications, but at least two years of directly relevant experience is still required |
| Timing | You may sit the exam before you meet the experience requirement, but you must apply for certification within five years of passing |
| Maintenance | 20 CPE credits per year and 45 USD annual maintenance for members, 85 USD for non-members |

**RECOMMENDATION.** CISA is the credential that changes your title in IT audit, not the one you buy first. Sit it when your day job already touches audit, controls, or assurance. If you are targeting a GRC analyst role, put CISA on the two-to-three-year horizon.

### Frameworks are not certificates

NIST does not certify people, and there is no NIST SP 800-63 credential. What you can show is fluency. Learn **NIST SP 800-63-4** (Digital Identity Guidelines, final July 2025) for identity assurance, **NIST CSF 2.0** for program structure, **NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5** and the Risk Management Framework for control implementation, and **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** for an ISMS. Then publish one control mapping or audit workpaper. That is the portfolio piece for this track.

**RECOMMENDATION: the sequence.** Security+, then GRCP, then GRCA if audit is the target, then CGRC once you have control or audit work, then CISA when your day job touches audit. CISA is a two-to-three-year goal, not a first purchase.

**Why this track is in this guide.** Most roadmaps stop at the tools. This one does not, because the author specializes in identity governance and access control: testing whether identity and access controls are actually implemented as described, not just documented. That is the seat where you check the tools, and it is an entry point, not a back office.

## The 2027 calendar

**This is a recommendation, not a rule.** It assumes roughly 10 hours a week and a full-time job.

**Q1 2027: Security+ (SY0-801).** Sit it by the end of March. Do not start studying for a second cert before this one is passed.

**Q2 2027: your track's first cert.** CySA+, AWS Cloud Practitioner, SC-300, Okta Certified Professional, or GRCP. Pick one. One.

**Q3 2027: the cert that changes your title.** AWS Solutions Architect Associate, Okta Certified Administrator, CySA+ if you started with something else, SailPoint if you are already in an IAM team, or CGRC once you have control work.

**Q4 2027: specialization or seniority.** SecAI+, AWS Security Specialty, PenTest+ (only with real experience), CISA only if your day job already touches audit, or SecurityX if you already hold a senior title.

**All year: two lab projects, documented.** One detection or incident writeup, one cloud, identity, or control-mapping build. Certifications get you the interview. The writeups get you the offer.

### If you have zero IT experience

**RECOMMENDATION.** Get any technical job first, including help desk, desktop support, or a NOC role. Then Security+ within your first year. The certification will not compensate for having never worked a ticket, and the ticket job is what gives you the stories you will tell in interviews.

### Five rules that decide whether this works

1. One certification at a time. Starting three is how people finish none.
2. Book the exam date before you feel ready. A date creates urgency that a study plan cannot.
3. Lab everything you read. If you cannot show it, you do not know it.
4. Read the official exam objectives, not just a course. The objectives are the exam.
5. Renewal is part of the cost. Every CompTIA cert on this list expires in three years and requires continuing education or a higher cert.

### On prices

CompTIA does not publish a single list price on its certification pages, and voucher prices vary with bundles, retakes, and region. Budget a few hundred US dollars per attempt and confirm the number at checkout on comptia.org. Academic pricing, exam bundles, and employer reimbursement are the three legitimate ways to reduce it.

### What this guide deliberately leaves out

Course-based certificates and badge-style credentials are not worthless, but they are not what hiring managers screen on for the roles in this guide. This roadmap stays with CompTIA, AWS, ISACA, ISC2, OCEG, and identity credentials that appear in job postings.
